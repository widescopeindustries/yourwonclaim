How VA Raters Actually Evaluate Your Claim

Every VA disability rating—regardless of condition—is based on three core factors. Raters are trained to look for evidence of these specific elements:

Factor What It Means What Raters Look For
FREQUENCY How often do your symptoms occur? Daily? Weekly? Constant? Episodic? Document specific patterns.
SEVERITY How intense are your symptoms when they occur? Mild, moderate, severe? Specific descriptions of pain levels, intensity of panic attacks, etc.
FUNCTIONAL IMPACT How do symptoms affect your daily life and ability to work? Can't work? Can't sleep? Can't maintain relationships? Specific limitations.

Why This Evidence Log Works: The template you're about to use is specifically designed to capture all three factors in a format that's easy for raters to process. When a rater opens your file and sees 30 days of documented symptoms with frequency, severity, and functional impact clearly recorded, they don't have to guess or interpret. The evidence speaks for itself.

Sleep Apnea: Condition-Specific Guidance

Sleep Apnea is rated under 38 CFR § 4.97, Diagnostic Code 6847. Unlike many conditions, sleep apnea ratings are heavily dependent on objective medical evidence (sleep studies) combined with documentation of required treatment and residual symptoms.

What C&P Examiners Focus On:

  • Sleep study results: AHI (Apnea-Hypopnea Index) score, oxygen desaturation levels
  • Treatment compliance: CPAP/BiPAP usage, hours per night, nights per week
  • Persistent daytime hypersomnolence: Excessive daytime sleepiness DESPITE treatment
  • Respiratory failure: Need for supplemental oxygen, CO2 retention
  • Functional impact: Falling asleep at inappropriate times, cognitive impairment, safety concerns

Sleep Apnea Rating Levels:

Rating Criteria
0% Asymptomatic but with documented sleep disorder breathing
30% Persistent daytime hypersomnolence
50% Requires use of breathing assistance device such as CPAP machine
100% Chronic respiratory failure with carbon dioxide retention, cor pulmonale, OR requires tracheostomy
CRITICAL FOR 50% RATING: The VA requires proof that you USE the CPAP, not just that you were prescribed one. Your Evidence Log combined with CPAP machine data downloads provides this proof.

How to Use the Evidence Log for Sleep Apnea:

Your log should document both your CPAP compliance AND your residual symptoms. Many veterans get stuck at 50% when they could qualify for consideration of additional ratings based on secondary conditions (like depression from chronic fatigue) or demonstrating that symptoms persist despite treatment.

Strong Entry Example: "CPAP usage: 6.5 hours (mask seal issues, woke twice to adjust). Still exhausted upon waking despite full night with CPAP. Fell asleep at desk at work around 1400—coworker had to wake me. Near-miss driving home—caught myself drifting in lane. Wife says I still stop breathing even with CPAP on (she counted 3 episodes). Headache most of morning. Had to cancel evening plans due to fatigue."

The strong entry documents compliance (6.5 hours), persistent symptoms despite treatment (exhausted, fell asleep at desk, near-miss driving), and functional impact (couldn't complete work tasks alertly, had to cancel plans). This demonstrates that the 50% rating criteria may not fully capture your impairment.

Weak Entry Example: "Used CPAP. Still tired."
