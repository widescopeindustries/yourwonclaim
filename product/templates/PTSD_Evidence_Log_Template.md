
# EVIDENCE LOG
## For PTSD (Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder) VA Disability Claims

### THE SMOKING GUN EVIDENCE
After years of navigating the VA claims process—as veterans ourselves—we discovered something that changes everything: **the single most powerful piece of evidence you can submit isn't officially part of a Fully Developed Claim.**

> **"No more convincing. Just corroboration."**

When you walk into a C&P exam without this documentation, the examiner's job is to **investigate whether your condition exists and how severe it is.** They're playing detective. You're trying to convince them.

When you walk in WITH a properly completed Evidence Log, the dynamic completely shifts. The examiner's job becomes **corroborating what you've already documented.** They're not investigating—they're verifying. That's a fundamentally different exam.

### HOW VA RATERS ACTUALLY EVALUATE YOUR CLAIM
Every VA disability rating—regardless of condition—is based on three core factors. Raters are trained to look for evidence of these specific elements:

| FACTOR | WHAT IT MEANS | WHAT RATERS LOOK FOR |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **FREQUENCY** | How often do your symptoms occur? | Daily? Weekly? Constant? With specific triggers? |
| **SEVERITY** | How intense are your symptoms when they occur? | Flashback intensity? Duration? Dissociative episodes? |
| **FUNCTIONAL IMPACT** | How do symptoms affect your daily life? | Can't work? Can't sleep? Avoid public places? Relationship breakdown? |

**Why This Evidence Log Works:** The template you're about to use is specifically designed to capture all three factors in a format that's easy for raters to process. When a rater opens your file and sees 30 days of documented symptoms with frequency, severity, and functional impact clearly recorded, they don't have to guess or interpret. The evidence speaks for itself.

**DO NOT FILE ANY CLAIM UNTIL THIS LOG IS COMPLETED AND INCLUDED WITH YOUR SUBMISSION**

### PTSD: CONDITION-SPECIFIC GUIDANCE
PTSD claims are evaluated under 38 CFR § 4.130, Diagnostic Code 9411. The VA uses the **General Rating Formula for Mental Disorders**, focusing on social and occupational impairment.

**Key Stressor Categories (38 CFR § 3.304(f)):**

| STRESSOR TYPE | EVIDENCE NEEDED | EXAMPLES |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Combat Engagement** | Lay statement alone is sufficient | Direct firefights, ambushes, patrols under fire |
| **Fear of Hostile Activity** | Lay statement + VA psych confirmation | IEDs, incoming mortar/rocket fire, convoy threats |
| **Military Sexual Trauma (MST)** | Behavioral change markers in records | Assault, harassment — look for transfer requests, performance drops |
| **Non-Combat Stressor** | Full corroboration required | Accidents, witnessing death in non-combat settings |

> **Critical:** For combat veterans and those who experienced fear of hostile military/terrorist activity, **your lay testimony alone can establish the stressor** if it is consistent with the places, types, and circumstances of your service. You do NOT need official records of the specific incident.

**What to Log Every Day:**
*   **Intrusion Symptoms:** Flashbacks, nightmares, intrusive memories — note triggers, duration, and intensity (1-10).
*   **Avoidance:** Places, people, conversations, or activities you avoided and WHY.
*   **Hyperarousal:** Hypervigilance, exaggerated startle response, sleep disturbance, irritability/anger outbursts.
*   **Negative Cognition/Mood:** Feelings of detachment, guilt, shame, emotional numbness, inability to feel positive emotions.
*   **Dissociative Episodes:** Lost time, feeling detached from reality, flashbacks where you "leave" the present.

**Rating Criteria:**

| RATING | IMPAIRMENT |
| :--- | :--- |
| **30%** | Occasional decrease in work efficiency due to depressed mood, anxiety, suspiciousness, chronic sleep impairment. |
| **50%** | Reduced reliability and productivity. Flattened affect. Panic attacks more than once a week. Difficulty understanding complex commands. Impaired judgment. |
| **70%** | Deficiencies in most areas (work, family, thinking, mood). Suicidal ideation. Obsessional rituals. Near-continuous panic/depression. Inability to establish relationships. Impaired impulse control. |
| **100%** | Total occupational and social impairment. Gross impairment of thought/communication. Persistent delusions or hallucinations. Persistent danger of hurting self or others. Inability to perform basic self-care. Disorientation. Memory loss for own name/occupation. |

### TIPS FOR MAXIMUM IMPACT
1. **Log EVERY day** — even "good" days. Write "Manageable today — mild hypervigilance at store, 3/10." Gaps look like you're exaggerating bad days.
2. **Use specific times** — "0300" is more credible than "nighttime."
3. **Name the trigger** — "Car backfire" is stronger than "loud noise."
4. **Quantify severity** — Use 1-10 scale consistently.
5. **Show functional impact** — "Missed work," "wife slept elsewhere," "kids asked what's wrong" — these are what drive ratings.
6. **Don't minimize** — Your worst days ARE your reality. Document them.
7. **Bring this log to your C&P exam** — Hand it directly to the examiner.
