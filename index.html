What I Discovered That Changed Everything

Most veterans file claims backwards. They throw everything at the wall – knees, back, tinnitus, headaches – hoping something sticks. Then they fight denials for years.

I did the opposite. I led with combat-related PTSD – the single most strategic claim you can make.

Here's Why Combat-Related PTSD is the Path of Least Friction:

  • No proof of stressor needed (if combat-related)
  • No nexus letter required
  • High value ($1,700+ per month average for severe ratings)
  • Fastest path to higher ratings
  • Can be filed as supplemental even if previously denied

And here's what most veterans don't realize: If you went through basic training, field exercises, or any military training for combat, you likely qualify for combat-related PTSD under VA regulations (38 CFR). This applies to veterans from ANY era – though those who served since 1990 are automatically classified as Gulf War era veterans with additional advantages.

This Isn't Your Grandfather's "Shell Shock"

Most people think PTSD means combat flashbacks and panic attacks. That's the old definition. Modern PTSD includes symptoms you might not even recognize as service-connected:

Hypervigilance

Always scanning rooms, sitting with your back to the wall, checking exits

Social Avoidance

Not wanting to go out in public, isolating yourself, avoiding crowds

Relationship Problems

Anger, emotional distance, difficulty connecting with family

Sleep Issues

Nightmares, insomnia, hyperarousal at night

Startle Response

Getting spooked easily, overreacting to sudden sounds

Irritability

Quick to anger, difficulty controlling emotions, constant tension

Sound familiar? That's service-connected PTSD. And there's a strategic way to claim it.

From No Claim to 100% in 13 Months

U.S. Army, 2001-2005 • Iraq Veteran

After four years in the Army including a year in Iraq, I came home changed. But like many veterans, I never filed a VA claim. I didn't want "handouts" or to get caught in the bureaucracy I'd heard horror stories about.

But as my symptoms worsened – the hypervigilance, the anger, the isolation – and started affecting my family and work, I realized I needed to act.

I approached my claim strategically. I didn't throw 15 conditions at the wall like most VSOs suggest. I focused on one solid, well-documented claim: combat-related PTSD.

I documented my symptoms honestly. I secured medical evidence. I crafted a personal statement that connected my service to my current struggles. And I understood the VA's own regulations about what qualifies as "combat-related."

Thirteen months later – after one initial claim, one supplemental, and one higher level review – I got the notification: 100% P&T approved.
