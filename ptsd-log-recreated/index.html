When you walk into a C&P exam without this documentation, the examiner's job is to investigate whether your condition exists and how severe it is. They're playing detective. You're trying to convince them.

How VA Raters Actually Evaluate Your Claim

Every VA disability rating—regardless of condition—is based on three core factors. Raters are trained to look for evidence of these specific elements:

Factor What It Means What Raters Look For
FREQUENCY How often do your symptoms occur? Daily? Weekly? Constant? Episodic? Document specific patterns.
SEVERITY How intense are your symptoms when they occur? Mild, moderate, severe? Specific descriptions of pain levels, intensity of panic attacks, etc.
FUNCTIONAL IMPACT How do symptoms affect your daily life and ability to work? Can't work? Can't sleep? Can't maintain relationships? Specific limitations.

Why This Evidence Log Works: The template you're about to use is specifically designed to capture all three factors in a format that's easy for raters to process. When a rater opens your file and sees 30 days of documented symptoms with frequency, severity, and functional impact clearly recorded, they don't have to guess or interpret. The evidence speaks for itself.

PTSD: Condition-Specific Guidance

Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder is rated under 38 CFR § 4.130, Diagnostic Code 9411. The VA uses a General Rating Formula for Mental Disorders that evaluates your condition based on occupational and social impairment. Understanding what raters look for is critical.

What C&P Examiners Are Trained to Document:

  • Re-experiencing symptoms: Nightmares, flashbacks, intrusive thoughts, emotional/physical reactions to triggers.
  • Avoidance behaviors: Avoiding people, places, activities, conversations that remind you of trauma.
  • Negative changes in mood/cognition: Memory problems, negative beliefs, detachment, inability to feel positive emotions.
  • Hyperarousal symptoms: Sleep disturbance, irritability, hypervigilance, exaggerated startle response, concentration problems.
  • Occupational impact: Difficulty maintaining employment, conflicts with coworkers/supervisors, missed work.
  • Social impact: Relationship problems, social isolation, difficulty maintaining friendships.

PTSD Rating Levels (What Your Log Should Demonstrate):

Rating Level of Impairment Symptoms Typically Present
0% Diagnosed but controlled Symptoms controlled by medication, minimal life impact.
10% Mild, transient symptoms Symptoms cause minor occupational/social impairment under stress.
30% Occasional decrease in efficiency Depressed mood, anxiety, suspiciousness, chronic sleep impairment.
50% Reduced reliability and productivity Flattened affect, panic attacks, difficulty understanding complex commands, impaired judgment.
70% Deficiencies in most areas Suicidal ideation, obsessional rituals, impaired impulse control, difficulty adapting, inability to establish relationships.
100% Total impairment Gross impairment in thought/communication, persistent delusions, persistent danger to self/others, inability to care for self.

How to Use the Evidence Log for PTSD:

When documenting your daily symptoms, be specific and honest. Don't minimize or exaggerate. The goal is to create an accurate record that a rater can easily cross-reference against the rating criteria.

Strong Entry Example: "Woke at 0300 from nightmare about [trigger event]. Heart racing, sweating. Could not return to sleep. Irritable with wife at breakfast—snapped at her over minor issue, immediately felt guilty. Avoided driving past [location] on way to work because it triggers anxiety. Difficulty concentrating at work—had to re-read same email 4 times. Left work early (1400) due to overwhelming anxiety. Isolated in bedroom rest of evening."

The strong entry captures frequency (woke at 0300, left work at 1400), severity (heart racing, sweating, overwhelming anxiety), and functional impact (snapped at wife, couldn't concentrate, left work early, isolated).

Weak Entry Example: "Bad day. Didn't sleep well. Felt anxious."
