The Smoking Gun Evidence

After years of navigating the VA claims process—as veterans ourselves—we discovered something that changes everything: the single most powerful piece of evidence you can submit isn't officially part of a Fully Developed Claim.

"No more convincing. Just corroboration."

When you walk into a C&P exam without this documentation, the examiner's job is to investigate whether your condition exists and how severe it is. They're playing detective. You're trying to convince them.

When you walk in WITH a properly completed Evidence Log, the dynamic completely shifts. The examiner's job becomes corroborating what you've already documented. They're not investigating—they're verifying. That's a fundamentally different exam.

How VA Raters Actually Evaluate Your Claim

Every VA disability rating—regardless of condition—is based on three core factors. Raters are trained to look for evidence of these specific elements:

Factor What It Means What Raters Look For
FREQUENCY How often do your symptoms occur? Daily? Weekly? Constant? Episodic? Document specific patterns.
SEVERITY How intense are your symptoms when they occur? Mild, moderate, severe? Specific descriptions of pain levels, intensity of pain, etc.
FUNCTIONAL IMPACT How do symptoms affect your daily life and ability to work? Can't work? Can't sleep? Can't maintain relationships? Specific limitations.

Why This Evidence Log Works: The template you're about to use is specifically designed to capture all three factors in a format that's easy for raters to process. When a rater opens your file and sees 30 days of documented symptoms with frequency, severity, and functional impact clearly recorded, they don't have to guess or interpret. The evidence speaks for itself.

Back Conditions: Condition-Specific Guidance

Back conditions (including lumbar strain, degenerative disc disease, herniated discs, and spinal stenosis) are rated under 38 CFR § 4.71a using either the General Rating Formula for Diseases and Injuries of the Spine or the Formula for Rating Intervertebral Disc Syndrome (IVDS). Ratings are based primarily on range of motion limitations and incapacitating episodes.

What C&P Examiners Measure:

  • Range of Motion: Forward flexion, extension, lateral flexion (both sides), rotation (both sides)
  • Pain on motion: Where in the range does pain begin? Does pain limit motion?
  • Flare-ups: How often? How severe? How long do they last? What triggers them?
  • Incapacitating episodes: Periods requiring bed rest PRESCRIBED by a physician
  • Neurological symptoms: Radiculopathy (pain/numbness/weakness radiating to legs)
  • Functional impact: Difficulty sitting, standing, walking, lifting, bending

Back Condition Rating Levels (General Formula):

Rating Range of Motion Criteria
10% Forward flexion greater than 60° but not greater than 85°; OR combined ROM greater than 120° but not greater than 235°; OR muscle spasm/guarding.
20% Forward flexion greater than 30° but not greater than 60°; OR combined ROM not greater than 120°; OR muscle spasm or guarding severe enough to result in abnormal gait or abnormal spinal contour.
40% Forward flexion 30° or less; OR favorable ankylosis of entire thoracolumbar spine
50% Unfavorable ankylosis of entire thoracolumbar spine
100% Unfavorable ankylosis of entire spine
THE FLARE-UP FACTOR: C&P exams capture your range of motion on ONE day. But the VA acknowledges that your condition may be worse during flare-ups. Your Evidence Log documents these flare-ups so the examiner can estimate additional functional loss during flares. This can increase your rating.

How to Use the Evidence Log for Back Conditions:

Document your daily pain levels, but more importantly, document what you cannot do because of your back. The VA rates based on functional impairment, not just pain. Also track flare-ups meticulously— when they happen, how severe, what triggered them, and how long they lasted.

Strong Entry Example: "Pain level 6/10 at rest, 8/10 with movement. Could not bend to tie shoes—wife had to help. Standing limited to 10 minutes before pain forced me to sit. Walking limited to approximately 100 yards. Could not lift laundry basket (~15 lbs). Shooting pain down left leg when sitting more than 20 minutes. Took 800mg ibuprofen at 0800 and 1400 with minimal relief. Had to lie down flat for 2 hours mid-day. Cancelled dinner plans because cannot sit in restaurant chairs."
FLARE-UP Entry Example: "FLARE-UP DAY 1: Woke with severe spasm after [trigger - yard work yesterday]. Pain 9/10. Cannot bend forward at all. Getting out of bed took 15 minutes. Called in sick to work. Spent most of day lying flat with ice/heat rotation. Could not prepare meals—wife brought food to bedroom. Pain medication barely touching it."
Weak Entry Example: "Back hurt today. Took some Advil."
